I’m Muzamil Mahdi Abdelhamid, a researcher passionate about molecular parasitology and parasite genome biology. I graduated in Veterinary Science from the University of Khartoum, Sudan, where I began my academic career as a teaching assistant at the Institute of Endemic Diseases. I later earned an MSc in Molecular Biology and undertook research training at the University of Cambridge, where I studied the genetics of Leishmania parasites. I then joined Biomedical Primate Research Centre (BPRC), the Netherlands, where I worked as a research assistant in malaria functional genetics, and vaccine development using the Plasmodium knowlesi–rhesus macaque model. This research formed part of my doctoral studies, leading to a PhD in Immunology.  After returning to the University of Khartoum, I focused on the molecular epidemiology and genomics of P. falciparum and P. vivax, mosquito vectors, molecular surveillance of antimalarial and antimicrobial resistance, and malaria diagnostics. Alongside my research, I taught molecular parasitology and immunology, led the Department of Parasitology and Medical Entomology, supervised MSc and PhD students, managed competitive international research grants, and I currently maintain collaborations with researchers worldwide. I have published over 80 manuscripts in international peer-reviewed journals. I joined the ParaFuGe lab at the University of Glasgow as an affiliate researcher, where I focus on the genome biology of P. falciparum. I’m interested in understanding how malaria parasites develop, replicate, and adapt, and how these processes can inform future research and interventions.
